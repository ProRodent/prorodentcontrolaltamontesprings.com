If we had to name the single most common entry point across every Altamonte Springs garage call, it would be the door sweep. That is the rubber or vinyl strip along the bottom of the garage door, meant to seal against the slab but one that rarely does after a few years of daily use.

Why garages are such an easy target.

A garage checks nearly every box a rodent looks for: shelter from weather, relative quiet, and often plenty of cardboard, stored fabric or holiday decorations that double as nesting material. Add a worn door sweep and the garage becomes one of the easiest rooms in the house to get into.

True basements are uncommon in this part of Florida, so the garage often plays a similar role to a basement in a northern home, a lower-traffic storage space attached to the house that gets checked less often than the kitchen or living room.

What to check yourself.

  • Stand inside the closed garage during the day and look for daylight at the bottom of the door.
  • Check the door's side seals, not just the bottom sweep.
  • Look at stored cardboard boxes for chew marks or shredded corners.
  • Check any gap where a dryer vent or utility line passes through the garage wall.

The fixes that actually help.

Replacing a worn door sweep is inexpensive and one of the highest-value fixes a homeowner can make between professional visits. Swapping stored cardboard for sealed plastic bins removes a lot of the nesting material that makes a garage attractive in the first place, even before any professional sealing work happens.

For gaps beyond the door itself, like utility penetrations or slab-to-wall joints, professional sealing with metal or hardware cloth holds up far better than a bead of caulk, which a motivated rodent can chew through in days.

When to call it in.

A simple seasonal reminder.

Because garage door sweeps wear down gradually rather than failing all at once, it is easy to not notice the gap growing until activity shows up. Checking it once a year, ideally before the busier fall months when attic and garage activity tends to climb in this part of Florida, catches the wear before it becomes an open invitation.

If you already have a professional inspection scheduled for another reason, mention the garage specifically; it takes only a minute to check during the same visit and often saves a second call down the line.

If your garage has a side service door in addition to the main vehicle door, check its weatherstripping and threshold the same way you would the main door sweep. Side doors are checked less often by homeowners and sometimes develop gaps unnoticed for longer.

Attached garages with an interior door leading directly into the home deserve particular attention to that connecting door's own seal, since it is the last line of defense once an animal has already gotten past the main garage door. A well-sealed interior door can be the difference between a garage-only problem and one that spreads into the living space.

A garage with a window should have that window checked for a secure seal too, since an older or poorly fitted garage window can be just as real an entry point as the main door itself.

A garage used as a workshop or hobby space, with more varied stored materials than a typical two-car garage, may need a slightly more thorough check given the wider range of potential nesting material and clutter involved.

Signs the garage needs a professional visit.

  • Droppings behind stored boxes or along the wall base.
  • Chewed wiring on stored holiday lights or equipment.
  • A door sweep replacement that does not stop new activity within a week or two.

What to do, and not do, while you wait

General safety practice drawn from CDC guidance on rodents. It is meant to keep you safe before any professional arrives.

  • Skip the broom and the vacuum on dry droppings. Stirring them up is how dust gets airborne.
  • Air out a closed attic, garage or crawl space for at least 30 minutes before you go in to work.
  • Put on gloves before touching anything contaminated, then wash your hands thoroughly when you finish.
  • Spray the area with disinfectant, wait roughly five minutes, then wipe it up with paper towels instead of dry material.
  • Double check that waste is bagged and sealed, and never pick up a dead rodent with bare hands.
  • Any fever, aches or breathing trouble after handling rodent waste should be checked by a doctor. We do not make health claims about individual exposure.

Common questions

How often should I replace a garage door sweep?

Check it at least yearly; replace it as soon as you see visible gaps, cracking or daylight showing through when the door is closed.

Is a detached garage different from an attached one?

The same entry points and fixes apply either way, though a detached garage may need its own separate check since it is not connected to the home's main structure.

Will a new door sweep alone solve an existing problem?

It closes one entry point but will not remove animals already inside; pair it with rodent trapping and removal if you already have signs of activity.

Do side service doors need the same attention as the main garage door?

Yes, a side door's weatherstripping and threshold wear the same way the main door sweep does, and it is checked less often, so it is worth adding to the same yearly review.
