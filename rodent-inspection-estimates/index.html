What an inspection covers.

An inspection is not a five-minute look in the attic. A technician walks the full exterior (roofline, soffits, garage door, utility penetrations) and the interior spaces most likely to show activity: attic, garage, kitchen and laundry baseboards, and any crawl space access.

The goal is a full list of entry points and activity signs before any pricing is discussed, because a plan built on a partial look almost always misses something and leads to a repeat call a few weeks later.

What you get at the end.

  • A plain-language list of where activity was found and how it is getting in.
  • A written plan covering trapping, sealing and any cleanup needed.
  • A price for that specific plan, explained before work begins.
You should know the plan and the price before a single trap goes down.

Why the house style matters.

Older homes near the center of Altamonte Springs tend to have different gaps than newer construction further out along SR 436, aging roof vents and block foundations versus tighter modern soffit returns. Mentioning the age and style of the house when you call helps the technician know what to look for first.

Estimates for exclusion and repair.

If the inspection finds chewed wiring, damaged insulation or soffit gaps, that gets priced as part of the same estimate rather than discovered later and billed as a surprise. You decide what to approve before anything is scheduled.

Scheduling.

Before the inspection.

  • Note when and where you have heard or seen activity.
  • Clear a path to the attic hatch and any crawl space access if you can.
  • Have a rough idea of the home's age and roof type.

Tell us on the call

  • The home's approximate age and roof type.
  • Where you have seen or heard activity.
  • Any recent roof, soffit or garage door work.

Before we arrive

  • Keep children and pets away from any area with droppings, a nest, or a suspected entry point.
  • Do not sweep or vacuum dry droppings; that can spread contamination into the air.
  • Do not set out your own loose bait or poison before the visit.
  • Note when and where you have seen or heard activity so the technician can start there.

Questions about rodent inspection and estimates

How is the inspection priced?

Ask about current inspection pricing when you call; either way, you are quoted and told the price before any treatment or sealing work is scheduled.

How long does an inspection take?

A full exterior and interior walk usually takes under an hour, longer if the attic access is difficult or the property is large.

Do you inspect commercial properties too?

Yes, the same inspection approach applies to shops, restaurants and offices; see our commercial rodent control page for details specific to businesses.
