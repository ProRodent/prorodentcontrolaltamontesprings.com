Why this part of Altamonte Springs needs rodent control

  • Older housing stock

    Many homes here were built decades ago, with roof vents and foundation gaps that newer construction codes have since tightened up.

  • Mature tree canopy

    Established oaks give roof rats an easy bridge from the yard onto older rooflines.

  • Attached garages common

    Garage door seals on older homes here are a frequent find during inspections.

Spring Valley's streets are some of the more established single-family blocks west of SR 436, with mature landscaping that has had decades to grow in around the houses. That maturity is good for shade and curb appeal, and it is also exactly what gives roof rats a bridge from yard to roofline.

On older homes the gaps we find during an inspection tend to cluster around aging roof vents and original garage door seals rather than the utility penetrations common on newer construction. Tell us the age of the house when you call so we know which to check first.

What inspections here usually find.

  • Roof vents and ridge caps original to the home's construction.
  • Garage door sweeps worn down from decades of use.
  • Tree limbs overhanging the roofline from mature yard trees.

Sealing older construction properly.

Sealing work on an older home sometimes means replacing an aging roof vent cover entirely rather than just patching it, since decades-old materials have usually already weakened. We explain honestly when a patch is enough and when the fix needs to go further.

A neighborhood where follow-up matters.

Because the tree canopy here keeps giving roof rats a route back toward the house even after a successful removal, a follow-up visit matters more in Spring Valley than it might in a newer, more open subdivision. We build that follow-up into the plan rather than treating it as optional.

Between visits, keeping hedges and mulch pulled back from the foundation and trimming shrubs off the walls removes the cover rodents use to approach the house, which matters most on established lots with mature landscaping.

Trees keep growing and roofing materials keep aging, so a roofline that was clear of limbs a few years ago may not be now. Checking for limbs within a few feet of the roof each season is a low-cost habit that closes the easiest route.

Tell us on the call

  • The approximate age of the house.
  • Whether any large trees overhang the roof.
  • The condition of the garage door seal.

Before we arrive

  • Keep children and pets away from any area with droppings, a nest, or a suspected entry point.
  • Do not sweep or vacuum dry droppings; that can spread contamination into the air.
  • Do not set out your own loose bait or poison before the visit.
  • Note when and where you have seen or heard activity so the technician can start there.

Questions about Spring Valley

Is this area worse for rodents than newer parts of the city?

The mature tree canopy and older construction standards here do mean more entry points on average, but every home still gets its own inspection rather than an assumption based on the neighborhood alone.

Should I trim my trees back from the roof?

If a limb is touching or close to the roofline, trimming it back is one of the most effective things a homeowner here can do between visits.

Are Spring Valley lots generally larger than average?

Many are, given the neighborhood's older development pattern, which is part of why yard landscaping around the foundation is worth checking alongside the usual roofline and garage inspection.
