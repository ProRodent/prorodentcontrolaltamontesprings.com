Why the yard matters as much as the house.

Outdoor rodent activity is often the warning sign that comes before an indoor problem. Burrows along a fence line, a shed with chewed-open bags of pet food, or rub marks on a retention pond bank usually mean the population is already established outside and looking for a way in.

Altamonte Springs' network of retention ponds, canals and drainage easements gives ground-dwelling rodents cover close to a lot of homes, which is part of why yard work is its own service rather than an afterthought to an indoor visit.

What gets checked outside.

  • Burrow openings along fence lines, under sheds and near foundation plantings.
  • Compost bins, pet food storage and bird feeder areas.
  • Overgrown hedges and vine cover touching the house or fence.
  • Tree limbs overhanging the roofline, a common roof rat bridge.
A burrow by the fence today is often the attic problem in a month.

Dealing with it at the source.

Outdoor bait stations, tamper-resistant and placed away from where children or pets play, are the main tool for yard-level activity. Burrow entrances get identified and treated rather than simply filled in, since a filled burrow with an active resident is usually reopened within days.

Sheds, compost and storage.

A shed with pet food, birdseed or fertilizer stored in anything other than a sealed metal or heavy plastic container is a near-guaranteed draw. We will point out where stored items are giving rodents an easy meal, even when the fix is as simple as swapping a paper bag for a sealed bin.

Seasonal patterns.

Outdoor activity tends to climb during Florida's rainy season, when flooding in low-lying yards and drainage areas pushes ground-dwelling rodents toward higher, drier ground, often a shed, a woodpile, or the base of a home's foundation planting.

After the visit.

Keeping the yard less inviting.

  • Store pet food and birdseed in sealed metal or heavy plastic containers.
  • Keep firewood and compost piles away from the house foundation.
  • Trim hedges and tree limbs back from fences and rooflines.

Before we arrive

  • Keep children and pets away from any area with droppings, a nest, or a suspected entry point.
  • Do not sweep or vacuum dry droppings; that can spread contamination into the air.
  • Do not set out your own loose bait or poison before the visit.
  • Note when and where you have seen or heard activity so the technician can start there.

Questions about backyard and outdoor rodent control

Do you treat yards that back onto a retention pond?

Yes, that is one of the more common property types we handle given how many Altamonte Springs lots border ponds, canals or drainage easements.

Is outdoor bait safe with pets in the yard?

Stations are tamper-resistant and placed away from areas pets use regularly; tell us where pets spend time outside so we can plan placement around that.

Should I fill in a burrow myself?

Filling an active burrow usually just gets reopened within days. Let us identify whether it is active before deciding how to treat it.
