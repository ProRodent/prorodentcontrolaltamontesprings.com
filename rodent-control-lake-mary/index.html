Call about Lake Mary

A newer city with a strong office corridor.

Lake Mary incorporated in 1973, making it younger than most of the other towns we cover, and its growth has leaned heavily toward office parks and planned residential subdivisions along the I-4 corridor. Newer construction can mean fewer aging original gaps, but it is not immune to rodent activity, especially around landscaped retention ponds common to planned developments.

Commercial accounts are a bigger share of our Lake Mary calls than in some of the older residential towns we cover, given the concentration of office parks.

What Lake Mary calls usually involve.

  • Office-park commercial accounts near the I-4 corridor.
  • Residential calls from planned subdivisions backing onto retention ponds.
  • Mouse activity through garage and utility penetrations on newer construction.

Newer construction, different gaps.

Newer homes and office buildings here tend to have tighter original construction. So when we do find an entry point, it is more often a utility penetration, an HVAC curb, or a gap that opened up after a later renovation, rather than an aging original vent.

Booking a Lake Mary visit.

Residential and commercial calls both route through the same 24-hour line. Give your address or business name and nearest cross street, and mention if documentation is needed for an office lease or property manager.

If several tenants in one office complex are affected, mention it on the call so visits can be planned together where the schedule allows, and so a landlord or property manager gets one consistent set of findings.

Lake Mary's planned-community layout also means many homeowners associations here have specific landscaping guidelines, so mention any HOA rules on tree trimming or yard changes if a prevention recommendation comes up during the visit.

On an office-park call we check dumpster enclosures, loading doors, break rooms and the ceiling voids above shared kitchens, since food areas are where commercial activity concentrates.

Before we arrive in Lake Mary.

  • Let us know if this is a residential or commercial address.
  • Note whether the property backs onto a retention pond.
  • Mention if documentation will be needed for a landlord or property manager.

Before we arrive

  • Block off access for children and pets to any area with droppings or a possible entry point.
  • Resist the urge to vacuum or sweep dry droppings; wetting with disinfectant first is safer, or just wait for the visit.
  • Make a note of the times and places you have noticed activity so far.

Tell us on the call

  • Your street address and nearest intersection.
  • The rough location of what you have seen or heard.
  • Whether pets or children are present in the home.
