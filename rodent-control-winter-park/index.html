Call about Winter Park

An established city with heavy tree cover.

Winter Park has been incorporated since 1887 and reincorporated as a city in 1925, and its reputation for mature oak canopy and brick-lined streets is well earned. That same canopy is exactly the kind of cover roof rats use to move from yard to rooftop, which makes roof rats the main concern on a Winter Park call.

The city's older, established neighborhoods sit alongside newer construction near its edges, so the entry points we find vary more within Winter Park itself than in some of the newer, more uniform subdivisions further out.

What Winter Park calls usually involve.

  • Roof rats bridging from mature oak canopy onto older rooflines.
  • Attic activity that has built up over time under dense tree cover.
  • Mouse entry through garage and foundation gaps on both older and newer homes.

Tree cover means the follow-up visit matters more.

Because the tree canopy here keeps giving rats a route back toward the roof even after a clean removal, we build a follow-up visit into every Winter Park job rather than treating it as optional. We will also point out specific limbs worth trimming back if we see an obvious bridge point.

Booking a Winter Park visit.

The same 24-hour line covers Winter Park addresses in ZIP 32792. Same-day visits are offered where the schedule allows.

Heavy shade keeps the roofline hidden under limbs, so rub marks, gnawed shingle edges or droppings along attic beams can show up before any noise does. If you see any of those signs, a check is worth scheduling even if nothing has been heard yet.

Before we arrive in Winter Park.

  • Note any oak limbs touching or close to the roofline.
  • Mention if the home is near a park, lake, or dense tree-lined street.
  • Keep pets away from any suspected attic access point.

A storefront or restaurant in the Park Avenue shopping district gets a check of the roofline, rear delivery area and dumpster placement, the same as a business near Altamonte Mall would.

Before we arrive

  • Block off access for children and pets to any area with droppings or a possible entry point.
  • Resist the urge to vacuum or sweep dry droppings; wetting with disinfectant first is safer, or just wait for the visit.
  • Make a note of the times and places you have noticed activity so far.
