Roof rats versus Norway rats.

In Central Florida, attic rat problems are most often roof rats, not Norway rats. Roof rats are excellent climbers that use tree limbs touching the roofline, power lines and vine-covered fences to reach the attic, which is why mature oak and palm canopy around older parts of the city correlates with heavier roof rat activity.

Norway rats are heavier, burrow at ground level, and show up more often around dumpsters, woodpiles and dense landscaping than in attics. Telling the technician whether the noise is overhead or at ground level helps narrow this down before the inspection even starts.

Finding the travel route.

A rat inspection traces the actual route from tree to roof to entry point, not just the attic itself. Rub marks along a branch, a gnawed gap at a roof-to-wall joint, or a worn path along a fence top are the evidence we look for outside before going up into the attic.

  • Tree limbs and power lines touching or overhanging the roofline.
  • Roof-to-wall gaps, gable vents and plumbing stacks.
  • Attic insulation disturbed into tunnels or flattened nesting areas.
A rat problem that keeps coming back usually means the tree limb never got cut back.

Removal and sealing.

Snap traps sized for rats go along the confirmed travel route inside the attic, and bait stations are used outside where children and pets cannot reach them. Once activity clears, the roofline gaps get sealed with metal flashing or hardware cloth, and we talk through whether a tree limb needs trimming back from the roof, since that is often the real long-term fix.

What a rat problem leaves behind.

Rats chew insulation into shredded nesting material and leave noticeably larger droppings than mice, often along the same beam or joist repeatedly. If the attic insulation looks flattened or tunneled in a specific area, that is usually the nest site, and we check there first.

Follow-up.

Signs the problem is resolved.

  • No fresh droppings on the same beam or joist after a week.
  • No scratching or thumping overhead at night.
  • Outside bait stations undisturbed on the follow-up visit.

Before we arrive

  • Keep children and pets away from any area with droppings, a nest, or a suspected entry point.
  • Do not sweep or vacuum dry droppings; that can spread contamination into the air.
  • Do not set out your own loose bait or poison before the visit.
  • Note when and where you have seen or heard activity so the technician can start there.

Questions about rat control

Why do roof rats do so well in Altamonte Springs?

The warm climate and mature oak and palm canopy around the city give roof rats an easy path from the trees onto rooflines year round, without the winter die-off that limits them further north.

Do I need to trim trees myself?

We will point out which limbs give rats a bridge to the roof, but tree work itself is outside our scope. Cutting that limb back is usually the single most effective long-term fix.

Are Norway rats common here too?

They show up, mostly around dumpsters, woodpiles and dense ground cover rather than attics, but roof rats are the more common attic problem in this part of Florida.

How fast do rats multiply if I wait?

A female rat can have several litters a year in a climate this mild, which is why we recommend not waiting once you confirm activity.
